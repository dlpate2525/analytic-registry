# V1.2 — Detection Coverage and Risk Buckets

Updated 21 October 2026. This describes the V1.2 prototype. The frozen 20 October baseline remains unchanged.

## Purpose

Detection Coverage helps administrators decide whether they have enough evidence to assess a concern. Risk buckets group findings by that concern.

Neither page proves compliance. The prototype displays saved illustrative findings; it does not run live platform detection.

Business High risk qualification remains separate: PRL 1 OR PII Yes OR EUCT Yes OR DMP Tier 1–3 qualifies. Qualification alone does not create work.

## Keep six meanings separate

| Term | Question answered |
|---|---|
| Dataset coverage | How much of the declared source scope was collected? |
| Detection coverage | Does this check have the evidence, applicable rule, and business context it needs? |
| Risk bucket | What kind of concern does this finding address? |
| Finding result | What did this particular assessment conclude? |
| Finding severity | How important is the issue? |
| Finding status | Where is the issue in its remediation lifecycle? |

A Review case also has a recorded work priority. Its priority and severity are displayed separately.

Complete inventory does not prove complete access or connection evidence. A zero finding count does not prove that controls passed.

## Evidence support labels

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Registry context needed | Business assignments, declarations, approvals, or an applicable rule are required. |
| Limited source evidence | The current profile supplies some relevant facts. It does not establish the full control result. |
| Not collected | The current primary query does not collect the relevant platform evidence. This is not a vendor capability judgment. |

These labels are independent of Complete, Partial, Failed, and NotCollected on a source delivery. They are also independent of Aligned or Unable to Verify on a finding.

The catalog describes the current primary profiles: Tableau repository queries, Alteryx Gallery exports queried in SQL Server, and landed Fabric inventory. Richer optional sources require their own verified mapping.

## Four themes, eleven retained buckets

Stored bucket names remain unchanged. Grouping makes them easier to navigate without rewriting historical findings.

| Theme | Bucket | Required interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Accountability | Orphan | Confirm a missing required business assignment or association. Partial source absence is inconclusive. |
| Accountability | Coverage / Bus-Factor | Review approved Champions, eligibility, and backup capacity. One Champion is permitted; limited backup is a warning. |
| Access & credentials | Access Risk | Compare effective access with approved requirements and applicable exceptions. |
| Access & credentials | Privilege Sprawl | Assess excess access against approved purpose and scope. Champion membership is not a publishing permission. |
| Access & credentials | Credential Exposure | Investigate verified credential use against an applicable rule. Unknown identity does not confirm exposure. |
| Governance | Compliance Gap | Review a specific applicable requirement and its supporting business evidence. |
| Governance | Bypassed Gate | Require an explicit approved gate and reliable release timing. Do not invent an asset approval or attestation gate. |
| Usage & capacity | Stale | Requires activity evidence and an approved expected cadence. Modification time is not usage. |
| Usage & capacity | Low Adoption | Requires usage, intended audience, a measurement period, and an agreed target. |
| Usage & capacity | Performance Risk | Requires runtime evidence and applicable workload thresholds. |
| Usage & capacity | Concentration Risk | Requires shared-resource use, a measurement period, and an approved threshold. |

Champion authority comes from effective approved assignments. Native group membership remains access evidence. Unresolved identity does not mean inactive.

## Operating flow

1. Collect source evidence with its original time, scope, and coverage.
2. Validate and accept the delivery through the registry operator.
3. Combine accepted observations with approved business context.
4. Assess only an applicable rule supported by sufficient evidence.
5. Track an issue through its explicit Review case and approved closure evidence.

Steps 2–4 describe the intended integrated process. The local Platform Imports page demonstrates it without importing data or running a detection engine.

A finding can retain several historical or current Review-case links. The interface follows explicit finding links only. Shared-case closure and reopening rules remain unresolved.

Completed case closure still needs an eligible Platform Manager, Platform Team, or Platform Owner approval for the exact recorded evidence. The current command retains its existing role rules. It does not infer a passed source control merely because a case closes.

## Page behavior

- Detection Coverage has platform and theme filters, one row per bucket, evidence details, and a next action.
- Administrator links open findings with the same platform and bucket, restricted to open issues.
- The dashboard uses the same evidence catalog. Historical sample capabilities do not produce current availability scores.
- Findings expose All statuses, Open, and Closed or resolved. Filters remain in the URL.
- Finding rows show Workspace and Asset and link explicitly associated Review cases.
- Review context shows Workspace, Asset, and Connection when recorded.
- Historical capability records remain stored and exportable. They do not drive the current catalog.

## Decisions left open

This refinement does not approve usage targets, runtime thresholds, evidence-age limits, retirement behavior, or new annual policy.

Shared-case closure and reopening need a separate decision. The interface preserves the current behavior and makes existing relationships visible.

Missing platform evidence stays unknown. Source observations cannot replace business responsibility, PRL answers, or approved business versions.
